1Arrive, brief occupants and assess—
  • Occupants, children or pets entering work area → trips, contact with exposed parts
  • Unexpected site conditions (damaged board, roof access) → work proceeds unsafely
M3×2
  • ISOBarricade switchboard area and roof access with bollards/tape; agree no-go zone with occupants
  • ADMINWalk the site before unloading; confirm scope and switchboard location with homeowner
  • ADMINTell occupants when power will be off and for how long
L1×2Dan Rossi
2Inspect switchboard backing for asbestos5 Asbestos
  • Meter panel may contain asbestos (common in pre-1990 SA homes) → inhaling fibres if drilled or broken
H3×4
  • ELIMDo not drill, cut or break the existing panel; if replacement needed, SA Power Networks / licensed asbestos removalist removes it
  • ADMINTreat any black/brown compressed-fibre board as asbestos unless tested CHECK: confirm house age / panel type
  • PPEP2 disposable respirator available if panel must be handled
L1×4Dan Rossi
3Isolate supply and prove de-energised12 Electrical
  • Contact with live parts → electric shock, arc flash, death
  • Supply re-energised by others while working
E4×5
  • ISOIsolate at main switch; arrange SAPN service-fuse withdrawal for board replacement CHECK: confirm SAPN booking
  • ISOLock out and tag isolation point; keep the key on you
  • ADMINTest-before-touch with a proved voltage tester (prove–test–prove) on every conductor
  • PPEInsulating gloves and arc-rated clothing for any testing near live parts
M1×5Dan Rossi
4Replace switchboard and fit new circuit12 Electrical 5 Asbestos
  • Residual live conductors (consumer mains, solar/battery backfeed) → shock
  • Sharp edges, manual handling of new enclosure → cuts, strain
H3×5
  • ISOIsolate and tag any solar inverter / battery (AC and DC) before opening board CHECK: confirm if solar/battery present
  • ENGInsulated tools and shrouding on any conductor that cannot be de-energised
  • PPECut-resistant gloves when handling enclosures and cable tray
M1×5Dan Rossi
5Work in roof space to run cable—
  • Heat in roof space (often 45 °C+ in summer) → heat stress
  • Falling through ceiling / off ladder → fractures
  • Existing live cables, vermin, loose-fill insulation → shock, bites, irritation
H4×3
  • ADMINSchedule roof-space work before 11 am on hot days
  • ENGWalk only on joists; use crawl boards across ceilings; portable LED lighting
  • ADMINMaximum 20 minutes in the roof space then a break with water; buddy stays at the manhole
  • PPEP2 respirator, long sleeves, gloves, eye protection
M2×3Dan Rossi
6Mount outdoor unit bracket at 2.4 m and run conduit1 Falls >2 m
  • Fall from ladder while drilling/fixing → head injury, fractures
  • Dropped tools/bracket → struck-by injury to people below
H3×4
  • SUBUse a mobile scaffold or platform ladder rather than a single-stile ladder CHECK: confirm access equipment
  • ENGLadder footed on firm level ground, secured at top, 1:4 angle; three points of contact
  • ISOExclusion zone below work area; no occupants within 2 m
  • ADMINTool lanyards; bracket lifted by second worker, not carried up the ladder
M2×3Dan Rossi / Liam Kerr
7Terminate, test and re-energise12 Electrical
  • Incorrect connection → shock or fire on re-energising
  • Re-energising while someone is still working
H3×4
  • ADMINComplete insulation resistance, polarity and earth continuity tests before energising
  • ADMINVerbal all-clear from every worker; remove own lock and tag last
  • ADMINIssue Certificate of Compliance (CoC) to owner
L1×4Dan Rossi
8Clean up and hand over—
  • Offcuts, dust, open manhole → trips and falls for occupants
M3×2
  • ADMINRemove all waste and offcuts from site
  • ADMINClose manhole, remove barricades last, walk the site with homeowner and explain new board
L1×2Dan Rossi
